01 · XML to LLM

An AI layer for a legacy ERP. No migration.

Tally stays the system of record. BillMiner sits beside it: XML comes out through custom reports, gets cleaned and indexed at the edge, passes through the readers and models, and goes back into Tally as XML it accepts.

Data out of the ERPResults back in, as XML Tally accepts
XML THAT DOESN'T HANG

Small, queued exports

Big Tally exports froze the billing PCs. Compact TDL reports and a 500 KB job cap keep sync running all day while staff bill.

TAX TO THE CENT

Rounds like Tally does

Tax is rounded half-up on each line, then summed, exactly as Tally computes it. Generated entries never fail over a one-cent rounding difference.

ZERO-TOKEN FAST PATH

Edge first, model last

Known aliases and defining words resolve in D1 in about 25 ms. Models are called only when the edge can't decide.

02 · Purchase scan

Two readers. Arithmetic picks the winner.

Every supplier bill is read twice in parallel. A reader's lines have to add up to the taxable total printed on the bill. When the two disagree, the one whose sums check out is used, not the one that sounds most confident.

Σ lines = printed taxable → match each line to a Tally item and a catalogue card (Gemma 4) → post

Regression on posted bills: 12 / 12 matched their posted totals and line counts.

Scan and post

The clerk scans a bill, fixes anything odd and posts it to Tally. A line with no Tally name never blocks the clerk.

Nightly diff on a Cloudflare cron

The scan's original words are compared with what was actually posted, to learn supplier aliases and per-supplier rules.

Guarded learning

Fragments are refused, notes are re-checked against the arithmetic, and every learned change can be undone.

An agent reviews each bill

A scheduled Claude agent audits the posted bill and fixes catalogue cards through logged endpoints that store the before and after state.

03 · Seller assistant + scan

From a WhatsApp photo to a Tally invoice.

Customers order the way they talk: a chat message, a forwarded email, a photo of a handwritten list. The seller assistant scans it, matches every line to an exact Tally item at the edge, and Rao writes the lines onto the bill for the clerk to confirm.

04 · Human in the loop

AI keeps the catalogue. People settle the doubts.

Thirteen thousand clerk-typed names don't stay clean on their own. Agents do the upkeep every day, and anything they can't prove goes to a person as one plain question. Every answer and every edit is logged, and every edit can be undone.

AI

Learns from each bill

A nightly job compares each scan with the posted invoice. A Claude agent then fixes the cards that bill touched.

AI

Researches the gaps

Makers' catalogues, PDFs and web search with two or more sources. The maker beats any single retailer.

Staff

Answers one tap

What only the shelf knows ("is this sold loose or by the box?") goes to a Questions tab on the staff phone.

Clerk

Teaches by working

Every pick or correction at the billing desk becomes an alias for next time. No training screen needed.

AI

Logs and can undo

Each card edit stores before and after. Wrong learning is rolled back, not argued with.

08 · In one page

What I built.

  1. Legacy ERP AI layer (XML to LLM) on Tally Prime: TDL/XML exports, a desktop sync agent and job queue feeding AI features with no change to how the accountants work.
  2. Purchase scan: a dual-reader bill pipeline (Vision OCR with layout rules, plus LlamaParse) reconciled against printed totals: 291 / 317 real supplier bills read and posted correctly.
  3. Seller assistant + scan: WhatsApp, email and handwritten-photo orders matched to exact stock items at the edge in ~25 ms per line (98% top-3); Rao writes them onto the invoice for the clerk to confirm.
  4. Human-in-the-loop catalogue upkeep: agents learn from every posted bill and research gaps; staff settle what only the shelf knows with one-tap questions; every edit is logged and reversible.
  5. 13,000 ERP names rebuilt into a family/variant catalogue under a nothing-is-lost rule, now powering a 1,914-product storefront.
  6. Under $5 a month to run the edge layer on Cloudflare Workers, D1 and KV; rules answer first and open-weight Gemma 4 handles the model work.
  7. Cent-exact sales tax matching Tally's per-line rounding, so generated invoices and tax returns reconcile.
  8. Open-weight migration: from frontier APIs to Gemma 4 in production, with a Gemma 2B LoRA ranking judge prepared.
